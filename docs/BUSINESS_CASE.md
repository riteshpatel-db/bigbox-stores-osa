Assumptions that link a dollar lever to a KPI (`business_case.yaml`, `links`):

1. A KPI moves by its backtest change x the kept share of its lever (lost-margin kept for availability and lost units, waste kept for shrink, carrying kept for inventory).
2. Recovered sales are valued at list price minus unit cost (gross margin), never at revenue; waste avoided lowers cost of goods one for one.
3. Losses scale with revenue - the same share of revenue at every company size (the modelled slice of 100 stores and 300 SKUs stands for the whole company).
4. Online short-shipped units fall by the same share as lost units (online orders ship from store stock).
5. The kept share equals the share of tonight's queue items that planners action as recommended.
6. Working capital is average inventory at cost; it changes by the backtest change in closing inventory at cost x the kept share.

## 6. Reconciliation with the value model in 03b

`evidence/03b_backtest_value.md` reports $11M/yr. It annualises the backtest's dollar deltas from the 8 held-out weeks (x6.52) using the generator's true lost sales, and keeps 50% of every lever. This case reports $7.1M/yr. It applies the backtest's percentage reductions to the full year's pipeline-measured cost and keeps 50% of lost margin, 40% of waste and 50% of carrying. The value per $ of run cost is 32 here against 50 in `evidence/07_cost.md`. Three things account for the gap:

- Over the holdout, the pipeline's lost-sales estimate in dollars is 74% of the true lost sales in the backtest's replay of the generator's own run (`03_ml.md` compares the same in units; here $4.00M vs $5.43M).
- The held-out weeks lose more than the average week: annualised, they come to 131% of the full year's estimate.
- Waste is kept at 40%, not 50%.

## 7. What would make these numbers wrong

- **The world is synthetic.** The baseline, the backtest and the policy share one generator, so real demand will be noisier, and real stockouts will have causes the simulator lacks (phantom inventory, backroom stock, planogram gaps).
- **The lost-sales baseline is an estimate.** Where truth exists (the holdout), the dollar estimate is 26% below it, which makes this case conservative. In production there is no truth to calibrate against.
- **Margin is at list price.** The measured average discount is 0.4%. At net price the lost-margin lever is 1% smaller.
- **8 weeks stand for a year.** The percentage reductions come from 56 days (2026-08-06 to 2026-09-30), with no peak season and no assortment change.
- **Linear scaling.** From $800M to $1.2B, the case assumes a bigger retailer loses the same share of revenue, with the same mix of slow movers and perishables as the slice.
- **The kept shares are guesses.** Adoption has not been measured: 7 app-session decisions, 0 of them approved as recommended. The mid case needs about half of tonight's queue actioned as recommended every night (link 5).
- **Planners may undo the waste gain.** On fast-moving perishables the ML policy's lost-units share rises from 0.8% to 2.9% in the backtest. Planners who see those stockouts will override upward, which is why waste is kept at less than half.
- **Units and dollars move in opposite directions.** The lost-units share rises (+0.3 pts in the backtest) while lost dollars fall, so the online fill rate, which counts units, drops slightly under link 4. A KPI owner who tracks units will see it get worse.
- **Working capital rises.** At service level 0.95 the policy holds more inventory, not less. A lower service level trades availability for inventory (03b shows the levels side by side).
- **Suppliers.** The backtest replays today's supplier draws (measured fill 94.9%, 10% of lines late). Extra orders that suppliers cannot fill lower capture.
- **Not valued (upside, or zero):** consolidating the two BI tools, intraday expediting, markdowns, planner labour and penalties. None of them is in the synthetic data.
- **Run cost is at list price, on a shared Large warehouse,** with cost_assumptions.yaml hours. Discounts lower it; heavier Genie use raises it.
